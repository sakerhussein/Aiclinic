# Jordan market, rules and booking evidence for Limitsiz Clinic Booking

Research date: 6 November 2026. Not legal advice. Items for the legal review are listed under Question 4.

Source labels: **[read]** means the page or document itself was opened. **[snippet]** means only a search summary was seen, because the page was blocked or not opened, so check before quoting. Vendor figures are marked as such and should not be treated as evidence.

## 1. Jordan private clinic context: how clinics book, market size, WhatsApp use, how patients pay

### Takeaway
There is no published count of private clinics, and no study of how Jordanian clinics take bookings. Those are the two biggest evidence gaps, and owner interviews will have to fill them. WhatsApp reach looks high but the best figure is old (78% of adults, about 2018). Private clinic spending is mainly out of pocket, and the insurance dispute of October 2026 has pushed more of it towards cash.

### Cited Findings

**Market size**
- No source gives a count of private clinics or of dermatologists or dentists in private practice.
- The Jordan Medical Association (JMA) president put cumulative registrations since its founding at 49,355 doctors, with 4,454 medical graduates in 2025 and only about 1,040 specialty training places — [Khaberni, snippet](https://www.khaberni.com/news/799341-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AF%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%B0%D8%B1-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B2%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%88%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%B5-%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%B5%D8%B5-%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%AF%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%A9). This is cumulative and includes doctors abroad, retired or dead, so it is not a market size.
- An older report put practising Jordanian doctors, in and outside Jordan, at 37,952 — [Roya, snippet](https://royanews.tv/news/326391). A separate undated government-sourced report put 25,176 doctors in the private sector — [Ad-Dustour, snippet](https://www.addustour.com/articles/1432555-%D8%B9%D9%80%D8%AF%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B7%D9%80%D8%A8%D9%80%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%81%D9%80%D9%80%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%80%D9%85%D9%80%D9%85%D9%84%D9%80%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%A3%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%8A). The search summary attributed these two numbers loosely, and neither is dated, so treat them as unreliable.
- The private sector mainly runs outpatient clinics, pharmacies, laboratories and 71 hospitals — [P4H, snippet](https://p4h.world/en/countries/jordan/).
- NatHealth, a third-party administrator (TPA), claims a network of over 10,000 providers including hospitals, medical centres and individual practitioners — [Jordan Financial Services, snippet](https://www.jordanfinancialservices.com/company-profile/nathealth). This is self-reported and undefined, but it is the only figure that hints at the number of provider sites.

**How clinics book today**
- No study or survey was found on the mix of phone, WhatsApp, paper and secretary in Jordanian clinics.
- Booking directories exist. Vezeeta Jordan lists doctors by specialty, area, insurer and fee, with payment at the clinic ([Vezeeta, snippet](https://jordan.vezeeta.com/SearchServicePage)); its doctor listing is titled 961 doctors ([Vezeeta, snippet](https://jordan.vezeeta.com/ar/%D8%AF%D9%83%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B1/%D9%83%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%B5%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%AF%D9%86)).
- Tebcan's listing is titled 2,210 doctors in Jordan ([Tebcan, snippet](https://tebcan.com/ar/Jordan/%D8%AF%D9%83%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B1/%D9%83%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%B5%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%AF%D9%86)). It filters by insurer, for example MedNet ([Tebcan, snippet](https://tebcan.com/ar/Jordan/%D8%AF%D9%83%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B1/%D9%83%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AE%D8%B5%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%AF%D9%86/%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%86%D8%AA)), and is reported to carry University of Jordan Hospital bookings ([Zawaya Plus, snippet](https://www.zawayaplus.net/archives/54585)).
- Walk-in culture shows up in hospital data. At King Abdullah University Hospital (KAUH) in northern Jordan, 30.61% of visits were without an appointment before a JD 15 fee was introduced, and 18.78% after — [Alyahya et al. 2016, read](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5798732). This is a teaching hospital with 65,535 patients, not a private clinic.
- In an online national survey in April 2024 (908 completers, recruited through Facebook, skewed to young, female, student respondents from Irbid and Balqa), 9% named difficulty making an appointment as a barrier to general care, and 25% said they were too busy to make time. Financial constraints were named by 6% — [Healthcare access survey, read](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13012521/). Authors state the sample is not representative.

**WhatsApp and digital use**
- Pew's report of March 2019: 78% of Jordanian adults use WhatsApp, 71% Facebook, and 85% a smartphone — [Pew, read](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2019/03/07/use-of-smartphones-and-social-media-is-common-across-most-emerging-economies/). The page does not state fieldwork year or sample; the linked dataset is labelled 2018.
- DataReportal Digital 2026: 10.6 million internet users (92.5%), 10.1 million mobile connections (87.7%), 6.80 million social media identities (59.2%), Facebook ad reach 5.55 million, Instagram 4.55 million, TikTok 2.34 million (18+) — [DataReportal, read](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-jordan). It publishes no WhatsApp figure for Jordan.
- Conflict: Techjury lists WhatsApp at 2.3 million against Messenger at 5.5 million in 2023 ([Techjury, snippet](https://techjury.net/research/market-share-of-messaging-apps-by-country/)), and Similarweb's Android chart ranks Messenger first ([Similarweb, snippet](https://www.similarweb.com/top-apps/google/jordan/communication/top-free/)). Both are unsourced or measure app rankings rather than use. Pew's survey is the better evidence, but it is old.
- No study was found of Jordanian doctors or clinics messaging patients on WhatsApp. The closest is a 2016 Beirut survey in which physicians used WhatsApp professionally but most saw medicolegal and privacy risk — [JMIR preprint, snippet](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/8895).

**How patients pay**
- Out-of-pocket payments were 36% of current health spending in the 2019 National Health Accounts, JD 806 million, with 90% of that in the private sector — [P4H, snippet](https://p4h.world/en/countries/jordan/). Other sources give about 28%, for different years and definitions — [Healthcare access survey, read](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13012521/).
- Health Ministry figures put insurance coverage at about 72% of Jordanians across all schemes, with civil insurance at 43% and 3.464 million subscribers — [Jordan Times, snippet](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/72-jordanians-covered-health-insurance-%E2%80%94-insurance-directorate). Private cover is estimated at 9% to 12.5% of the population, from older sources.
- Figures from the 2015 census era are much lower, about 55% to 56% of the total population — [DoS, snippet](https://dosweb.dos.gov.jo/DataBank/Analytical_Reports/Health_Insurance_in_Jordan.pdf). The gap is probably partly definitional (Jordanians versus all residents).

### Inferences
- The realistic buyer is a doctor-owned clinic where most money is self-pay and part is insured. Civil insurance users mostly go to public facilities, so the 72% figure overstates who can use private clinics on cover. The 9% to 12.5% private cover figure is closer, but both are weak.
- With about 2,000 doctors on the largest directory and tens of thousands registered, digital booking appears thin. This is a rough signal, not a measured adoption rate.
- A doctor surplus (4,454 graduates against about 1,040 training places) suggests rising competition among young doctors, which could increase demand for patient acquisition. This needs testing.
- The product should expect walk-ins as normal. A booking system that cannot log a walk-in will not match the day as it runs.

### Gaps
- Number of private clinics, dermatologists and dentists: no source. Requests to the JMA, the Jordan Dental Association (JDA) and the Ministry of Health licensing department would be the route. The DataReportal 2026 WhatsApp figure is not published for Jordan.
- Share of clinics using phone, WhatsApp, paper or software: no source. Suggest 15 to 20 owner and secretary interviews plus a count of WhatsApp numbers on clinic Google Maps listings.
- Current nationally representative WhatsApp use: the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission (TRC) household survey was not found.
- Consultation fees in JOD: not found. Clinic cash-versus-card share: not found.

## 2. Payments in Jordan: CliQ, eFAWATEERcom, gateways, and fit for deposits and payment links

### Takeaway
CliQ is the mass-market rail, with 167.9 million transactions in 2025, and it has a request-to-pay feature that suits deposits in principle. However, no public business API or payment-link product was found, so integration depends on a bank or acquirer. Card gateways offer the most ready link products, but their Jordan pricing is unpublished.

### Cited Findings
- JoPACC 2025 report: CliQ 167.92 million transactions (+100%) worth JD 19.97 billion (+65%), 2.13 million registered users (+27.3%). eFAWATEERcom 75.43 million transactions worth JD 15.99 billion, about 81% digital. JoMoPay wallets 102.04 million transactions worth JD 6.34 billion. Mobile wallet users 2.81 million at end of 2025, 46.8% women. Instant payment user base 3.93 million — [Int@j, read](https://intaj.net/jopacc-releases-its-2025-payments-systems-report/). Secondary report of the JoPACC report; the original was not opened.
- Early 2025 monthly data: transfers were 84.9% of CliQ transactions and purchases 15.1% — [Jordan Times, snippet](https://jordantimes.com/news/business/jd26b-money-transfers-cliq-first-2-months-2025). No full-year merchant share was found.
- Since 1 October 2025 banks charge merchant, corporate and business accounts a fixed fee that varies by transaction size. Each bank sets the amount, transfers between individuals stay free, the single-transfer limit stays JD 10,000, and the Central Bank monitors fees — [Roya News, read](https://en.royanews.tv/news/63722/Jordan-introduces-fees-on-%E2%80%98CliQ%E2%80%99-transfers-for-merchants,-businesses). The article does not publish the tiers.
- CliQ request-to-pay lets a payee send a payment request that the payer accepts or rejects, with instant transfer on acceptance — [JoPACC CliQ services, snippet](https://www.jopacc.com/what-we-do/systems-platforms/instant-payment-system-cliq/cliq-services).
- CliQ QR payment at merchants exists through Network International on the POS device — [JoPACC, snippet](https://www.jopacc.com/media-center/news/jopacc-collaborates-network-international-launch-instant-qr-payment-services). CliQ is domestic and JOD only — [JoPACC FAQ, snippet](https://www.jopacc.com/faqs/cliq-faqs). JoPACC's FAQ page blocked direct reading.
- HyperPay does not publish its rates ([HyperPay blog, snippet](https://www.hyperpay.com/blog/)). Third-party guides quote card fees of 2.5% to 3.5% and setup of JD 100 to 500 ([PayAtlas, snippet](https://payatlas.com/countries/jordan-jo)), and HyperPay at 2.5% to 3.2% plus annual fees ([Weblix, snippet](https://weblix-jo.com/en/blog/payment-gateways-jordan-guide-2026)). Unverified; neither names a data source.
- PayTabs serves Jordan, including through a Shopify app — [Saree3, snippet](https://saree3.io/blog/shopify-in-jordan.html). The Jordan guide also says licensed wallets offer acceptance accounts to individuals with lower ceilings — [Weblix, snippet](https://weblix-jo.com/en/blog/payment-gateways-jordan-guide-2026).
- WhatsApp's business policy bars asking people to share full card numbers, financial account numbers or personal ID numbers in chat — [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy, read](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/).

### Inferences
- Average CliQ value was about JD 119 per transaction (19.97 billion over 167.92 million, my calculation), so a deposit of JD 10 to 30 is well inside normal use.
- With 2.13 million registered CliQ users among roughly 11.5 million residents (10.6 million internet users at 92.5%), adult reach is meaningful but far from universal. Cash must remain an option. Users may overlap with the 3.93 million instant payment base.
- Simplest first version: send the clinic's CliQ alias in WhatsApp, patient pays, and staff mark it as paid. That needs no integration, but staff must check a bank app. It should be tried before building any API work.
- Card gateways give a true payment link and automatic confirmation, but they likely cost setup fees plus 2.5% or more, and they cannot be justified for small deposits without confirmed pricing.
- eFAWATEERcom looks suited to recurring bills from registered billers, not one-off clinic deposits. This is an inference; nothing was found on clinic use or biller onboarding.
- Payments must happen outside the chat, through a link or the patient's bank app, so the agent never asks for card details.

### Gaps
- Whether any bank or provider offers clinics a CliQ request-to-pay API or link: not found. Ask Network International Jordan, Arab Bank and a payment service provider directly.
- Actual Jordan quotes from HyperPay, PayTabs and MEPS: not published.
- Refund and reversal rules on CliQ: JoPACC FAQ could not be read.
- eFAWATEERcom biller requirements, and tax treatment of deposits on medical services: not found.
- Whether patients in Jordan accept deposits at all: no evidence found.

## 3. Insurance: how private clinics work with insurers and TPAs; is booking-time verification realistic?

### Takeaway
Automated verification at booking is not realistic now. No public TPA API was found, and since 18 October 2026 the JMA has told private doctors to stop dealing with four insurers, including MedNet, and to take cash with a receipt. The insurers are also studying an end to medical networks altogether. The product should capture insurer details and show the clinic's own accepted list, not promise live eligibility checks.

### Cited Findings

**How the system works**
- NatHealth describes itself as the largest TPA in Jordan, with over 12 million claims processed and more than 400,000 beneficiaries — [Kawar Group, snippet](https://kawar.com/our-sectors/healthcare). It reports a 24/7 pre-approval centre and an e-health gate for paperless approvals — [Jordan Financial Services, snippet](https://www.jordanfinancialservices.com/company-profile/nathealth). Self-reported marketing.
- MedNet Jordan offers provider network, authorisation and claims management, and selects and monitors clinics and doctors — [Jordan Financial Services, snippet](https://www.jordanfinancialservices.com/company-profile/mednet-jordan).
- Med. Service reportedly manages the Ministry of Health insurance fund — [Jordan Financial Services, snippet](https://www.jordanfinancialservices.com/company-category/third-party-administrators).

**The dispute (this matters most)**
- The JMA published a new fee schedule in the Official Gazette in October 2024. It raises fees 60% over the 2008 schedule in phases of 20% after one month, one year and two years, and up to 300% in some cases. The Insurance Federation threatened court action — [Al-Araby, read](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/economy/%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A3%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%AF%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%AD%D8%B5-60-%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%B7-%D8%AE%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%81-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A3%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86). The JMA later described the rise as roughly 20% to 40% over 2008 — [FactJo, read](https://factjo.com/news/151017?slug=alatbaaa-tshtrt-alaltzam-blayh-alagor-lanhaaa-alkhlaf-maa-altamyn-151017). These figures conflict.
- An agreement was signed on 15 June 2025. The JMA says insurers did not honour it, so from 18 October 2026 it stopped dealing with four insurers: مدكسا, MedNet, Al-Oula and Newton — [Maqar, read](https://maqar.com/archives/774461); [Ad-Dustour, snippet](https://www.addustour.com/articles/1597291). One source spells the first name Medex; the Arabic transliteration should be confirmed.
- The decision covers private-sector doctors and is called a first phase. The doctor treats, collects cash and gives a receipt. Emergencies, hospital admissions and intensive care are exempt — [FactJo 14 October, read](https://factjo.com/news/150879?slug=nkab-alatbaaa-tokf-taaaml-alktaaa-alkhas-maa-4-shrkat-tamyn-othyk-1791994665). Patients take the receipt to their insurer — [Maqar, read](https://maqar.com/archives/774461).
- The Insurance Federation and the Jordanian Health Insurance Association warn of legal action against doctors who refuse insured patients, including removal from networks. They are studying ending the network system in favour of pay-and-claim — [Maqar, read](https://maqar.com/archives/774461).
- JMA conditions include: full use of the 2024 schedule, payment straight to doctors' bank accounts, a joint technical committee, and insurers accepting claims through the doctors' Cooperative Fund platform and the "Hakeem" system — [FactJo 17 October, read](https://factjo.com/news/151017?slug=alatbaaa-tshtrt-alaltzam-blayh-alagor-lanhaaa-alkhlaf-maa-altamyn-151017). It is unclear whether this means the national Hakeem programme or another platform.
- No reports later than 17 October were found, so the position on 6 November may have changed.

### Inferences
- For now, the useful feature is a clinic-maintained list per doctor: insurer, status (accepted / cash and receipt / not accepted), and an effective date, so the WhatsApp agent can answer "do you take my insurance?" correctly. Status must be easy to change in a week, given how fast this is moving.
- A "cash receipt for insurer" print or send step gains value during the dispute. It is cheap to build, and it would keep its value if insurers do move to pay-and-claim.
- If networks end, insurance would matter less at booking, and cash collection and receipts would matter more. If the dispute settles, pre-approval tracking may matter. This argues against investing now in TPA integration.
- WhatsApp policy (Question 4) also bars asking for ID numbers in chat, so collecting policy numbers should be done with care, and ID numbers should be kept out of the chat.
- For dermatology, much cosmetic work is likely not insured, which reduces the relevance further. This is an inference; no source was found.

### Gaps
- Whether any TPA offers a provider API or eligibility check to outside software: not found. NatHealth's e-health gate is the place to ask.
- How much of a typical clinic's income is insured, the clinic's actual pre-approval steps, payment delays: no source.
- Whether dentists are affected: the dispute reports concern the JMA; the JDA's position was not researched.
- Whether Insurance oversight now sits with the Central Bank: the JMA wrote to the Central Bank and the trade ministry, but the supervisory rules were not reviewed.

## 4. Rules: data protection, health data, advertising and patient messaging, medical records, Hakeem, and WhatsApp platform rules

### Takeaway
The data protection law is in force, with the compliance period over since 17 March 2025. It has a healthcare exception to consent, but appointing a data protection officer and transferring data abroad are open issues for a WhatsApp and AI product. The largest risk found is not Jordanian law: WhatsApp's business policy bars sending or requesting health-related information, which needs a product decision and legal review. Marketing messages also fall under JMA advertising instructions in force since 1 February 2025.

### Cited Findings

**Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)**
- Timeline: published 17 September 2023, in force 17 March 2024, full compliance 17 March 2025 — [MoDEE presentation 2025, read](https://www.modee.gov.jo/EBV4.0/Root_Storage/AR/PDPL_Presentation_Website_2025.pdf); Articles 1 and 23 of the [official English text, read](https://www.modee.gov.jo/ebv4.0/root_storage/en/eb_list_page/pdpl.pdf). Article 3 applies the law to data collected before it took effect.
- All article references below come from that MoDEE English text. The Arabic text governs and should be checked.
- Article 2: sensitive data includes health and physical or mental condition.
- Article 5: consent must be explicit, documented in writing or electronically, and specific on duration and purpose. A parent or guardian consents for someone lacking legal capacity, which matters for paediatrics.
- Article 6(A)(2): processing without consent or notice is allowed if necessary for preventive medicine, medical profiling, or the provision of healthcare by a licensed practitioner.
- Article 6(B): data must not be kept after the purpose ends, unless legislation says otherwise.
- Article 9 requires notice before processing, subject to Article 6. Article 12 limits processors to the set purpose and period.
- Article 11(A): a data protection officer is required when processing sensitive data, among other triggers. No size or healthcare exception appears.
- Article 14: transfer to any other person needs consent, and transfer for marketing needs specific consent. Article 14 does not state that it is subject to Article 6.
- Article 15: no transfer abroad to a recipient with lower protection, except listed cases including medical data needed for treatment and consent after being told of the weak protection. Article 15(B) requires checking the recipient's protection first. Article 17 has the Council publish a list of adequate countries.
- Article 20: serious breach means notifying affected people within 24 hours and the Unit within 72 hours of discovery.
- Article 21: warning, then licence action or a fine up to JD 500 a day, capped at 3% of prior-year revenue. Article 22: fine of JD 1,000 to 10,000, doubled on repeat, and a court may order destruction of the data.
- Article 17 and Article 24 provide for licences and permits, with an exemption to be set for entities processing for the original purpose. Article 18 provides a registry of controllers, processors and officers.

**Implementing rules (status unclear)**
- A Jordanian law firm, dated 29 October 2025, says Regulation No. 28 of 2025 on Disclosure of Data and Regulation No. 68 of 2025 on Data Subject Rights have been issued, with MoDEE breach instructions, pre-consent and withdrawal forms, and DPO accreditation criteria — [Atwan Attorneys, read](https://www.atwanattorneys.com/page.php?page_id=22). Gazette references are not given.
- Cabinet approved a regulation on the Unit's working mechanisms in 2025 — [Khaberni, snippet](https://www.khaberni.com/news/756771-%D9%85%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%8A%D9%82%D8%B1-%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84-%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%AE%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%A9-2025).
- Conflict: a compliance vendor's 2025 guide says the Council designation is still pending ([ConsentStack, snippet](https://www.consentstack.io/regulations/jo-pdpl)). DLA Piper's page, modified 8 February 2026, names no specific instruments ([DLA Piper, read](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=JO)). I found no 2026 Council decisions, adequacy list or enforcement actions.
- DLA Piper reads Article 14 as allowing electronic marketing before consent. The Article 14 text speaks of transfer between parties, and Article 14(A)(3) needs consent for transfer for marketing. This is an open question.

**Medical advertising**
- JMA instructions on medical advertising and media were published on 24 December 2024 and took effect on 1 February 2025, under Article 35(l) of the JMA Law No. 13 of 1972 — [Al-Wakeel, read](https://www.alwakeelnews.com/Section_1/%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A_714402). Provisions below are from this report:
  - A committee monitors advertising and refers violations for discipline. Websites and social media need its approval, at a 10 JD licence for websites.
  - Discounts, offers and free or reduced treatment are barred unless the committee approves. Advertising instalment plans outside the facility is barred.
  - Before-and-after images of the same patient need written consent. Images during surgical or cosmetic procedures are banned.
  - Superlatives such as "first" or "only" are banned, as is promoting drug or cosmetic products.
  - Advertisements must be in Arabic. Other languages may appear only as matching subtitles.
  - Doctors and facilities are personally responsible for what appears on their pages.
- The JMA is developing a further system on social media advertising, which would be mandatory, and a draft was reported to be at the Legislation Bureau — [Jordan Times, snippet](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/new-jma-system-crack-down-misleading-medical-advertisements-social-media); [Jordan Times, snippet](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/jma-publish-list-licenced-doctors-rein-misleading-ads-malpractice%C2%A0). Pages were blocked; dates and status are unconfirmed.
- Dentists have a separate system, the JDA advertising and media system with instructions of 2022 ([JDA, snippet](https://www.jda.org.jo/index.php/component/k2/item/1838-%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87-%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9-2022.html)). A legal database reports advertisements valid for 30 consecutive days or five separate times a year, from council approval ([Jordanian Law, snippet](https://jordanianlaw.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%85%D8%A9/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%8A-%D9%84%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A3%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%86/)). Not checked in full.
- Separately, the food and drug regulator (JFDA) controls drug advertising, requiring prior approval for non-prescription drugs — [Arab News, snippet](https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/jordan-announces-stricter-regulations-on-prescription-drug-advertisements-2567136).
- No Ministry of Health rule specifically on patient messaging or clinic reminders was found.

**Medical records and Hakeem**
- The Ministry of Health issued a policy on electronic health records, patient information protection and e-prescriptions in July 2013; a patient rights text dates from 1998, updated 2014 — [Policy brief, snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7031133/). No source sets record content or retention for private clinics.
- Hakeem, run by Electronic Health Solutions (EHS), connected 482 facilities as of 7 July 2026, all under the Ministry of Health and Royal Medical Services — [Petra, read](https://petra.gov.jo/index.php/en/news/hakeem-system-expands-to-cover-482-healthcare-facilities-so-far-ceo). The article does not mention private facilities.
- The Private Hospitals Association is listed among EHS stakeholders — [EHS, snippet](https://ehs.com.jo/electronic-health-solutions-ehs). No evidence was found of private clinics connecting to Hakeem, or of any public integration route.

**WhatsApp platform rules**
- Section 3 of the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy says: "Don't use WhatsApp for telemedicine or to send or request any health related information." — [WhatsApp policy, read](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/).
- Section 4 lists medical and healthcare products, and drugs, as prohibited; Section 5 sets limits for regulated verticals. Section 1 requires opt-in, and best practice is separate opt-in by message category. Section 2 requires a clear route to a human when automating — [same source](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/).
- Since 15 January 2026 the WhatsApp Business terms bar providers whose AI is the primary functionality; business bots serving a wider business purpose are reported to stay allowed — [Dig.Watch, snippet](https://dig.watch/updates/meta-changes-whatsapp-terms-to-block-third-party-ai-assistants); [respond.io, snippet](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban).
- Meta charges per delivered template message by category and market since July 2025. Jordan falls under Rest of Middle East, whose marketing rates rose on 1 October 2026. From that date each number gets 1,000 free service messages a month, and service replies, including from third-party AI, can be charged beyond that — [Meta developer docs, read](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing). The Jordan rates themselves are in a rate card I did not open.

### Inferences
- A booking and reminder message can be kept free of health content: name, date, time, doctor, clinic. Reason for visit, symptoms and procedure names are where the WhatsApp clause bites, and for dermatology the service name itself may reveal health information. "Booking agent that asks what's wrong" and "triage over WhatsApp" are at risk. The policy's exact scope is not clear from the text, and many clinics and vendors use WhatsApp this way; I found no enforcement data.
- Reminder and booking messages appear to fit Article 6(A)(2), while recall, reactivation and promotional messages probably do not. It would be safer to log separate marketing consent, which also matches WhatsApp's opt-in advice.
- Any broadcast with an offer may count as advertising, needing committee approval and Arabic-first text. The product should not ship "promotion campaigns" as a default for clinics without a warning.
- A sole-doctor clinic that holds health data seems to need a DPO on the plain text of Article 11. Limitsiz could offer a simple compliance pack, which is a differentiator only if it is correct.
- Unit cost of the AI agent depends on message pricing. Long chats may now cost money past the free tier, so rate-card testing is needed.
- Hakeem integration should not rank high: no evidence of a route exists.

**Items for the legal review**
1. WhatsApp health-information clause: what content is safe, and whether to redesign the agent around it.
2. Whether Limitsiz is a processor or a controller, and who is responsible for Meta and AI model providers as further parties.
3. Whether Article 6(A)(2) covers reminders sent through a third-party vendor, and whether Article 14 consent is still needed.
4. Whether servers, AI models and Meta outside Jordan meet Article 15, given no adequacy list was found.
5. DPO duty for small clinics, and accreditation.
6. Whether licences, permits or registry entries (Articles 17, 18, 24) apply to Limitsiz or clinics.
7. Current regulations and Council status, checked in the Official Gazette: Regulations 28 and 68 of 2025 and the Unit regulation.
8. Whether recall or follow-up messages count as healthcare, marketing, or advertising under JMA and JDA rules.
9. Retention periods for records, including the Medical and Health Liability Law of 2018 and the Public Health Law, which were not retrieved.
10. Consent for minors in paediatrics, and how before-and-after images may be stored.

### Gaps
- Text of the Medical and Health Liability Law and the Public Health Law on confidentiality and retention: not retrieved. The search did not return them.
- Ministry of Health rules on clinic advertising, and any rule on patient messaging or electronic record standards for private clinics: not found.
- Any enforcement action under the PDPL: not found.
- Current status of the JMA social media system: pages blocked.
- Whether Meta has enforced the health clause against clinics: not found.

## 5. Evidence: no-show rates and what cuts them

### Takeaway
Reminders have strong evidence, with Cochrane and later reviews agreeing, and a large 2026 WhatsApp emulation found one reminder about 24 hours before was as good as two. Shortening the wait to the appointment and targeting calls at high-risk patients also have support. Evidence for deposits and overbooking is weak, and no trial of waitlists was found. Only one verified Jordanian study exists, in a teaching hospital.

### Cited Findings

**Jordan**
- KAUH (527-bed teaching hospital, 39 outpatient clinics, 65,535 patients, September 2014 to September 2015): a pre-post study of a JD 15 fee for unscheduled visits. Mean missed-appointment rate per patient fell from 45.12% to 41.27% (P < .001). Unscheduled visits fell from 30.61% to 18.78%. Younger, married and male patients missed more often, and patients in the lowest copay group attended more. No control group and six months of follow-up — [Alyahya et al. 2016, read](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5798732). This is a per-patient average, not a rate per appointment, so it is not comparable with the rates below. It is also not a no-show fee.
- A Jordanian vendor blog estimates 10% to 20% for clinics — [Barmajtek, snippet](https://clinic.barmajtek.com/blog/reduce-clinic-no-shows-ar). Marketing estimate, no data. No study of private Jordanian clinics was found.

**Middle East**
- Oman: 14 primary care facilities, ophthalmology, ENT and dermatology clinics, mean non-attendance 55.6% against a cited global primary care mean of 23%. Risk factors were male sex, young age, new appointments, early-morning slots, Ramadan bookings and long lead times. Rates fell as the appointment system bedded in — [Oman Daily report of Oman Medical Journal study, read](https://www.omandaily.om/%D8%B9%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%85/na/%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AE%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%B9%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%8A%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%88%D8%B2-50). Newspaper report; original paper not opened. Public, free care.
- Saudi Arabia: 21% at a Riyadh adult rehabilitation department (336 no-shows), with illness that day 24% and distance or ambulance need 17%. The paper cites a national range of 11% to 30% — [AlOsaimi et al. 2025, snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12041131/). An older Riyadh public hospital study had transport at 26.4% and forgetting at 13.7% — [Riyadh study, snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11412932_Missed_appointments_at_public_hospitals_in_Riyadh_Saudi_Arabia).
- Qatar: Hamad Medical Corporation specialty clinics 32%, well clinics 22% — [Al-Arab, snippet](https://alarab.qa/article/28/11/2024/%D8%AA%D8%BA%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%B6%D9%89-%D8%B9%D9%86-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%AF%D9%87%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D9%86-%D9%8A%D8%AA%D8%AD%D9%85%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%80%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A4%D9%88%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9).
- Israel: about 15% in a public hospital's ambulatory clinics — [Israeli hospital study, snippet](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6664577/).
- Saudi SMS trial (King Fahad Teaching Hospital, Youssef 2014): 517 of 700 attended with SMS, 508 of 799 without, about 74% against 64% (as tabulated in the review below) — [Al-Turbag et al. 2026, read](https://jhmhp.amegroups.org/article/view/10215/html).

**Reminders**
- Cochrane review (Gurol-Urganci, 2013; search to August 2012): attendance 67.8% without reminders, 78.6% with text, 80.3% with phone call. Low to moderate quality evidence that text increases attendance and matches phone calls, at lower cost per attendance in two studies — [Cochrane review PDF, snippet](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/1386850/1/CD007458.pdf).
- Robotham et al. 2016, BMJ Open: 26 articles, 21 in the primary analysis (8,345 with notifications, 7,731 without). Multiple notifications beat single ones, and voice appeared better than text in a secondary analysis — [Semantic Scholar, snippet](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Using-digital-notifications-to-improve-attendance-Robotham-Satkunanathan/a68086634bbd21a439342b7bd43ef7376779cfa5).
- Al-Turbag et al., published March 2026 (search to October 2023): 10 RCTs, 8,236 participants, hospital outpatients. Attendance RR 1.11 (95% CI 1.05 to 1.19), I² 83%. Telephone RR 1.11 (1.04 to 1.19), five studies. SMS RR 1.14 (0.99 to 1.31), four studies, not significant, I² 91% — [Al-Turbag et al. 2026, read](https://jhmhp.amegroups.org/article/view/10215/html).
- Werner et al. 2023, BMC Health Services Research: 61 studies, 56 on reminders, consistently linked to fewer no-shows, with the best channel varying. Other behavioural approaches were rarely tested — [Europe PMC record of Werner et al., read](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1186/s12913-023-10059-9&resultType=core&format=json).
- WhatsApp: target trial emulation, Buenos Aires public system, 475,214 appointments in 15 specialties, June 2023 to May 2024, control no-show 34.6%. Single 24-hour reminder RR 0.76 (0.72 to 0.81), risk difference -8.21 points. Dual 72 and 24 hour RR 0.80 (0.79 to 0.81), -7.05 points. Single 72-hour RR 0.91 (0.84 to 0.99), -3.16 points. All raised patient cancellations, dual most — [medRxiv preprint, read](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26360609v3). Not peer reviewed and not randomised. This is the only WhatsApp study found, and it disagrees with Robotham on multiple reminders.
- Wording: UK trials found stating the appointment's cost in SMS reminders reduced missed hospital appointments — [Hallsworth et al. 2015, snippet](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0137306). NHS context; effect sizes not checked.

**Lead time and scheduling**
- Dermatology: University of Pennsylvania, seven sites. Acuity-driven blocks (7, 21, 90 days), with unused slots opened two days before. No-shows fell from 18.6% to 8.1% and new patient lead time fell 76.8% — [Healio on NEJM Catalyst 2026, read](https://www.healio.com/news/dermatology/20260924/call-center-decision-tree-model-cut-dermatology-wait-times-reduced-noshow-visits). Single system, before and after, no confidence intervals. The system had far more demand than slots.
- A US dermatology case-control study of 99,476 encounters found lead time and prior no-show history to be the main predictors, with younger age and distance — [Dermatology study, snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369597606_Understanding_Barriers_to_Medical_Appointment_Keeping_A_Case-control_Study_of_Predictive_Factors_for_No-shows_and_Same-day_Cancellations_in_Dermatology_Clinics_in_an_Academic_Medical_Center_in_the_Uni). Ophthalmology no-shows rose from 9.1% at 0 to 2 weeks to 38.3% at 6 months — [Ophthalmology study, snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274396901_Lead_time_for_appointment_and_the_no-show_rate_in_an_ophthalmology_clinic). A systematic review also names prior no-shows as the top predictor — [Systematic review in Entropy, snippet](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/22/6/675).

**Targeted calls, overbooking, waitlists**
- JAMIA rapid review of predictive model interventions: targeted text probably effective; phone calls median RR 0.61 from 3 RCTs (4,959 appointments); effect of model-based overbooking uncertain — [JAMIA 2023, snippet](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/30/3/559/6889491).
- Calling patients with predicted risk of 15% or more on top of automated reminders reduced in-person no-shows in a randomised quality initiative — [JGIM 2023, snippet](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-023-08209-0).
- No trial of waitlist backfill or same-day cancellation filling was found.

**Deposits and fees**
- No randomised study of deposits or prepayment was found, in any setting.
- Danish RCT: 6,746 first orthopaedic appointments, May to November 2015, fine about €34. Non-attendance about 5% in both arms, cost difference not significant — [Blæhr et al., BMJ Open 2018, read via abstract record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Blaehr%22%20AND%20fine%20AND%20BMJ%20Open&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=3). The baseline was very low, and 79% of 130 fines stayed unpaid after reminders — [search summary of Blæhr table, snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-effect-of-fine-on-cancellation-and-non-attendance-n6746_tbl2_324531518).
- Israeli policy review, 2023: research on fines is limited and mixed. The effect depends on the amount, collection and service. Authors expect a booking co-payment law to have little effect, and warn about inequality — [Israel J Health Policy Res, read via abstract record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1186/s13584-023-00575-8&resultType=core&format=json).
- MGMA survey data: practices with a no-show fee reported more improvement (25% against 16%) — [vendor blog, snippet](https://pabau.com/blog/how-to-improve-patient-no-show-rate/). Observational, vendor-relayed, and the search summary did not make clear which vendor page carried it, so check before use.

### Inferences
- Suggested order of value, from the evidence: 1) a WhatsApp reminder about 24 hours before, with one-tap confirm or cancel; 2) capture of cancellations, since reminders convert no-shows to cancellations; 3) booking near-term slots, with limits on how far ahead; 4) a staff call list for high-risk patients: first visit, long lead time, prior no-show, young male; 5) filling freed slots, on logic alone; 6) deposits as a clinic option for long or costly procedures, not a default; 7) no automatic overbooking.
- Effect size to promise: an absolute cut of about 5 to 10 points at a high baseline. At a baseline near 10% the gain will be smaller, perhaps 2 to 3 points. Vendor claims of "up to 80%" have no support in these sources. Each clinic should measure its own baseline first, since no private clinic baseline exists.
- Reminder design choice: WhatsApp gets a single 24-hour reminder as the default, with an optional earlier one for long or costly procedures only, since it may trigger cancellations, which is useful only if the slot can be refilled.
- Almost all Middle East figures come from free or public care. Private clinics, where the patient chose and is paying, may see lower rates, and this needs measuring.
- A deposit is a larger behaviour change than a fine, and would also filter bookings. It might do its work in cosmetic dermatology, but that is untested.
- Ramadan and early-morning effects from Oman suggest reminders and limits should be time-aware.

### Gaps
- Any no-show rate for private clinics in Jordan: none found. A search summary gave a KAUH figure of 24.9% with dermatology at 14.32%. It was attributed to a paper that turned out to be from Buenos Aires ([Giunta & Alonso Serena 2019](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Nonattendance%20rates%20of%20scheduled%20outpatient%20appointments%20in%20a%20university%20general%20hospital%22&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=3), 27.84% non-attendance). These figures could not be verified and must not be used.
- Reasons for no-show in Jordan: not reported in the KAUH paper.
- Evidence on cosmetic dermatology or private dental no-shows in the region: not found.
- A published randomised trial of WhatsApp reminders: not found. RCT evidence for deposits and for waitlists: not found.
- Cochrane has not been updated since 2013.

## 6. Secretary and front-desk workload: what takes their time, and independent evidence on reducing it

### Takeaway
No Jordanian or Arab evidence exists on clinic secretaries, and I found no modern peer-reviewed time study anywhere. Qualitative UK work shows that new channels can add work unless they replace old ones. Independent evidence that AI agents reduce front-desk load was not found. Local time-and-motion observation would be new evidence and useful for sales.

### Cited Findings
- No study of Jordanian clinic secretaries' workload was found.
- The only time study that surfaced is from 1972 (Buchan and Richardson, cited in a protocol) — [Systematic review protocol, snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5651636/). That protocol concerns clinical roles of receptionists; its findings were not found.
- UK general practice, qualitative: receptionists handle several channels at once, online services have been absorbed into their role without patients knowing it, and staff report rising workload — [BJGP 2025, snippet](https://bjgp.org/content/75/759/e703).
- UK focused ethnography: telephone consultations added tasks for receptionists, such as recording reasons and checking contact details, with unintended extra work — [BJGP 2018, snippet](https://bjgp.org/content/68/672/e478).
- Modelling: digital-first access could raise general practice workload unless strict conditions are met. This concerns GP workload, not receptionists — [JMIR 2020, snippet](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7327596/).
- Mayo Clinic web self-scheduling of well-child visits: no-show rates were not harmed, and some patients preferred phone for complex cases — [Retrospective study, snippet](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8077742/).
- COVID testing, one US health system: about 57 nurse calls per 100 staff-scheduled appointments, suggesting scheduled volume drives phone work — [Retrospective analysis, snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9465581/). Specific to testing.
- Registered US trial of AI-assisted scheduling for well-child visits reports 16% escalation to humans, with disclosure of automation at the start — [NCT06698640, snippet](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06698640).
- Preprint on an LLM scheduling agent: 91.8% task completion and 96.0% safety compliance, on synthetic scenarios only — [arXiv 2607.05055, snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05055).
- Vendor figures, unverified: about 8 minutes per scheduling call, attributed to Accenture via Phreesia; 80 to 120 daily phone interactions, attributed to MGMA; 30% to 40% turnover, attributed to MGMA 2024 — [Strello, snippet](https://strello.health/blog/reduce-front-desk-workload); [Medical front-desk post, snippet](https://www.letsaskclaire.com/healthcare/medical-front-desk-staffing-crisis). A 27% drop in scheduling requests at Sutter Health, attributed to an AMA case study, and "60% fewer calls" repeat through blogs — [US Tech Automations, snippet](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/healthcare-patient-self-scheduling-how-to-2026). Not usable as evidence.

### Inferences
- The UK findings suggest a real risk: adding a WhatsApp agent to phone, walk-ins and the secretary's own WhatsApp may add a channel to monitor, not remove one. The staff inbox and clear handover matter as much as the AI itself.
- With 16% escalation in the one registered example, plan for about one in six automated attempts needing a person. This comes from a different setting, a US well-child trial, and Arabic dialect is likely to be harder.
- Secretaries are likely to be the daily users and may fear for their jobs. Pitching the product as help for her, rather than replacement, seems safer. No local evidence supports this; it is a reasoned guess.
- Suggested pilot measure: one week of tally counts in two clinics before and after, covering calls, WhatsApp messages, confirmations, no-shows, and time spent on each. Results would be stronger than anything in the literature for this market.

### Gaps
- Time-use figures for clinic reception anywhere, in peer-reviewed form: not found.
- Independent controlled evidence comparing AI agents with staff on call volume or workload: not found.
- Secretary salaries, turnover and number per clinic in Jordan: not found. The value of time saved cannot be priced yet.
- Arabic dialect performance of booking agents in live patient use: not found.
