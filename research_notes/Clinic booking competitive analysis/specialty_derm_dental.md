# Specialty scheduling and practice software for dermatology, aesthetic and dental clinics (as of November 2026)

Research date: 6 November 2026. No site was signed in to and no form was submitted.

Evidence labels used below:
- **Read** = the page itself was fetched and read.
- **Search summary** = taken from a search result summary; the page was not opened in full. Check it before quoting it externally.
- **Vendor claim** = produced by a company that sells the software or a service around it. Competitor pages (for example Pabau writing about rivals) are flagged as such.

## Dermatology and aesthetics: what do specialty systems offer that a general calendar does not?

### Takeaway
The core difference is that a booking holds more than a doctor and a time. It also holds a room and a device, it draws on a prepaid course of sessions, and it needs signed consent. Package tracking, consent linked to a service, and room or device checks are documented in vendor help centres. Commission and interval-based recall are not well evidenced in what I could read.

### Cited Findings

**Room and device scheduling**
- Pabau: rooms and equipment are set up under Setup > Services > Resources. By default an item of equipment can be used for all services; turning that off links it to chosen services and locations. The article does not mention a quantity field or an equipment-specific conflict check (Read) — [Pabau equipment help](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/resources-rooms/equipment)
- Pabau: if a laser service can only run in rooms holding the laser machine, staff cannot move the appointment to a room not linked to that service. Moves also fail if the other room is unavailable or allocated to another staff member's shift (Search summary) — [Pabau room change restrictions](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/why-room-changes-are-restricted-for-appointments)
- Pabau online booking: a slot shows only if the staff member and at least one linked room are free, and a free room is then assigned at random (Search summary) — [Pabau room availability in online booking](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-manage-room-availability-in-online-bookings)
- Mangomint: "Resources" represent rooms, equipment and other limited items. Adding a resource requirement to a service prevents double-booking. A service can need only a resource and no staff member (the example is a sauna). The calendar shows which resources are in use (Read) — [Mangomint resources help](https://www.mangomint.com/learn/help-articles/calendar/resources.md)
- Mangomint says resources are allocated automatically each time such an appointment is booked (Search summary, vendor claim) — [Mangomint blog](https://www.mangomint.com/blog/resource-scheduling/)
- Zenoti: equipment is linked to a service and its availability is checked whenever that service is booked; a room and equipment can both be linked to one service; there is an equipment revenue report. The post appears to date from about 2016 (Search summary) — [Zenoti upgrade post](https://www.zenoti.com/blog/your-april-upgrade-is-here-2)
- Zenoti marketing describes automatic room and device assignment for laser bookings (vendor claim) — [Zenoti laser page](https://www.zenoti.com/en-uk/medical-spa-software/laser-treatments)
- Pabau treats "procedure-based scheduling", where a template holds duration, room and equipment for each procedure, as a buying criterion for dermatology systems (competitor-authored ranking, Search summary) — [Pabau dermatology EHR article](https://pabau.com/blog/7-best-dermatology-ehr-software/)
- Boulevard: I found no source confirming room or equipment scheduling. This is a gap in my reading, not evidence the feature is missing (see Gaps).

**Session packages and courses of treatment**
- Pabau tracks and deducts sessions automatically. The client's Packages tab lists active and expired packages with redeemed and remaining counts, and usage history shows date, service and practitioner (Search summary) — [Pabau package tracking](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-track-and-manage-package-sessions)
- Pabau repeat booking from a package: staff open "Book from Package", choose a repeat option, and the sessions are added to the calendar. The count is limited by remaining sessions and the package expiry date. The article does not list intervals or say how clashes are handled (Read) — [Pabau repeat appointments](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-repeat-appointments-for-package-sessions)
- Pabau clients can see remaining, used and booked sessions in a portal and book with a package. A package is only valid for listed services and until expiry (Search summary) — [Pabau client portal package booking](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-book-an-appointment-using-a-package-in-the-client-portal)
- Pabau staff can redeem several sessions for one appointment at checkout (Search summary) — [Pabau multi-session redemption](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-redeem-multiple-package-sessions-for-a-single-appointment)

**Memberships, retail and included scope**
- Pabau lists scheduling, online booking, client portal, forms, payments/POS, memberships, marketing automation, inventory, reporting and team roles on every plan (Read) — [Pabau pricing page](https://pabau.com/pricing/)
- Mangomint lists "Memberships & Packages" and "Forms & Charting" in its included features, though the page does not state explicitly that no extra fee applies (Read) — [Mangomint pricing](https://www.mangomint.com/pricing/)
- Boulevard offers monthly or annual auto-renewing memberships and custom service packages (Search summary, vendor claim) — [Boulevard medspa features blog](https://www.joinblvd.com/blog/3-favorite-boulevard-medspa-features)
- AestheticsPro lists AP Photo, point of sale, inventory, staff management, e-records and a form creator on all editions, and a library of 500+ customisable forms (Read) — [AestheticsPro pricing](https://www.aestheticspro.com/Software-Pricing/)

**Before-and-after photos and consent**
- Pabau's guidance is that every form should be linked to at least one treatment or consultation service (Search summary) — [Pabau medical form best practices](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/medical-form-best-practices)
- Pabau marketing says consent forms and questionnaires can be sent before each treatment and chased automatically if incomplete (vendor claim, Search summary; the page is on a Pabau staging host) — [Pabau digital forms](https://teststaging.pabau.com/features/digital-forms)
- Pabau's consent template has separate checkboxes for clinical records, before-and-after photos, staff education, marketing, social media and research (Search summary) — [Pabau photo release template](https://pabau.com/templates/photo-release-form/)
- Pabau's guide says marketing use of patient photos needs its own authorisation, not just general treatment consent. This is a vendor's reading of US HIPAA, not legal advice (Search summary) — [Pabau photo consent guide](https://pabau.com/blog/before-and-after-photo-consent-form-medical-spa-requirements/)
- Boulevard charting lets staff upload photos, mark injection sites and record products and quantities (Search summary, vendor claim) — [Boulevard medspa features blog](https://www.joinblvd.com/blog/3-favorite-boulevard-medspa-features). A competitor says Boulevard lacks advanced before-and-after workflows — [PatientNow comparison](https://www.patientnow.com/resources/compare/med-spa-boulevard-alternatives). The two conflict, and both have commercial interests.
- Nextech is described as offering dermatology and cosmetic templates, advanced photo management and consent forms; ModMed as offering specialty templates, photo management and mobile access. These come from third-party comparison sites, not vendor documents (Search summary) — [Software Finder: Nextech vs ModMed](https://softwarefinder.com/resources/nextech-vs-modmed); [Mindbowser buyer's guide](https://www.mindbowser.com/?p=119290)

**Follow-up cycles**
- Clinic pages mostly give laser hair removal intervals of 4 to 6 weeks for the face and 6 to 8 weeks for the body, and 6 to 8 sessions. One UK clinic gives a different pattern. I found no dermatology society guideline in these results (Search summary, clinic marketing) — [Dermapure timeline](https://www.dermapure.com/en/timeline-laser-hair-removal/); contradicted by [a UK clinic page](https://www.colaz.co.uk/how-many-sessions-for-laser-hair-removal/)
- A 2008 literature review studied ideal interval and session count, but its abstract did not give the figures in what I saw — [PubMed 18330795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18330795/)

**User sentiment**
- Aesthetic Record: 3.5 from 75 verified reviews on Capterra, with sub-scores of 3.4 to 3.7. One June 2025 reviewer in New Zealand reports customers unable to book for 5 days after go-live, which is a single anecdote (Search summary) — [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/162938/Aesthetic-Record/reviews)
- Aesthetic Record on G2: 5.0, but from only 3 reviews, so weak evidence. Its summary mentions checkout glitches (Search summary) — [G2 reviews](https://g2.com/products/aesthetic-record-aesthetic-record/reviews)

### Inferences
- Limitsiz's rule of "one booking per doctor at a time" is not enough for laser and device work. A clinic with one laser and two doctors can double-book the machine while both doctors' calendars look free. This is the single largest gap in dermatology.
- The Mangomint design (a resource is a thing, a service lists what it requires) is the simplest pattern to copy, and it fits how Limitsiz already models services with durations.
- The WhatsApp agent must check the same resource rule as staff booking. Pabau's online rule (doctor free and at least one linked room free) is a good starting point.
- Interval spacing should be set per service by the clinic, because the published intervals vary and depend on the patient.
- The "repeat from package" flow in Pabau is the model to beat: choose a course, generate the remaining sessions, stop at remaining count and expiry. Pabau's article does not explain clash handling, so showing clashes clearly is a possible point of difference. That is untested.
- Commission, retail and memberships look like billing-system features. Limitsiz has no payments today, so these depend on a separate product decision.

### Gaps
- Commission: I found no reliable documentation for Pabau, Boulevard or others. Do not claim anything about how they calculate it.
- Boulevard room and device scheduling, and which plan tiers include memberships, packages or commission: not confirmed. Its pricing page does not say.
- ModMed EMA and Nextech: I read no vendor documentation. They are aimed at medical dermatology with insurance billing, and their fit for small clinics in Jordan is unknown.
- Zenoti: no current help article found, and the equipment post is old. Its help centre did not appear in my searches.
- I found no evidence of how any of these systems handles Arabic or RTL. Pabau's page does not mention it. Absence of evidence here is not proof.
- Vendor-driven "treatment-plan recall", meaning a system that automatically reminds a patient 6 weeks after a laser session, was not found documented in a help article. Marketing automation is listed by Pabau but I did not read how it is triggered.

## Dental: what do specialty systems offer that a general calendar does not?

### Takeaway
Dental booking is organised around chairs (operatories), recall due dates and the treatment plan held in the chart. The most reusable ideas are recall as a working list with due dates, and a report of planned treatment that has no appointment. Insurance estimates are heavily tied to US-style benefit data and are unreliable even in the established products.

### Cited Findings

**Chair scheduling and recall**
- Open Dental's setup requires providers and hygienists, operatories assigned to default providers, and provider schedules assigned to operatories, plus Web Sched operatories if online booking is used (Search summary, primary manual) — [Open Dental recall manual](https://opendental.com/Manual/recall.html)
- Open Dental's default recall types are Prophy, Child Prophy and Perio. The interval, procedures, appointment length and due date are configurable, and a patient can be set to perio as needed (Search summary, primary manual) — [Open Dental recall types](https://opendental.com/Manual/recalltypes.html)
- Open Dental's Recall List includes every active patient for the default types. Scheduling from it searches for openings after the due date. For a family it searches after the last family member's due date and across all their providers (Search summary, primary manual) — [Open Dental recall list](https://opendental.com/Manual/recalllist.html)
- Manual changes to a patient's interval are kept when recall is synced, though the due date may be recalculated (Search summary, primary manual) — [Open Dental recall types](https://opendental.com/Manual/recalltypes.html)
- Patient self-booking of recall is sold as an add-on: Web Sched Recall is $75 a month per location, and eConfirmations is $25 a month (Read, November 2026) — [Open Dental fees](https://www.opendental.com/site/fees.html)

**Treatment plans linked to booking**
- Dentrix's Unscheduled Treatment Plans report finds plans in the chart with no appointment in the Appointment Book. It also shows remaining insurance benefits so staff can prioritise. The Scheduling Assistant has a similar view (Search summary, vendor blog) — [Dentrix Enterprise blog](https://blog.dentrixenterprise.com/following-up-on-your-unscheduled-treatment-plans)
- Dentrix Ascend documents searching for openings from a treatment plan (Search summary; title only) — [Dentrix Ascend learning](https://learn.dentrixascend.com/tag/treatment-plans/)
- A third-party tutorial says staff attach planned procedures to an appointment from the Appointment Book's Tx button, and Dentrix may warn about an unscheduled or broken appointment (Search summary) — [Docsie Dentrix tutorial](https://www.docsie.io/tutorials/dentrix/create-a-treatment-plan/)

**Insurance estimates and eligibility**
- Dental Intelligence's help says estimates on treatment plans sent from Dentrix Ascend are not accurate and staff may need to calculate them by hand (Search summary) — [Dental Intel workflows](https://intercom.help/dental-intel/en/articles/5740819-pms-specific-treatment-plan-workflows)
- A Dentrix user request asks for per-procedure estimated coverage in the unscheduled report, suggesting it is not available there (Search summary) — [Dentrix Ideas](https://dentrix.ideas.aha.io/ideas/DTX-I-8032)
- Curve Dental reviewers praise claims and eligibility but one review summary calls billing and payments confusing (Search summary) — [GetApp Curve reviews](https://www.getapp.com/healthcare-pharmaceuticals-software/a/curve-dental/reviews/); [G2 comparison](https://www.g2.com/compare/curve-dental-curve-dental-vs-dentrix)

**Recall interval evidence**
- NICE guideline CG19 recommends adjustable adult recall from 3 to 24 months according to risk, not a fixed six months for everyone (Search summary) — [NICE 2020 surveillance](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/cg19/resources/2020-exceptional-surveillance-of-dental-checks-intervals-between-oral-health-reviews-nice-guideline-cg19-pdf-11678930994373)
- The UK INTERVAL trial found no evidence of an oral health difference between six-monthly, risk-based and 24-month recall over four years (Search summary) — [NIHR summary](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/dental-check-ups-every-six-months-unnecessary-people-low-risk-oral-disease)

**Regional products**
- Dentolize is an Egyptian dental product. A Cairo press feature describes an AI tool that drafts follow-up messages in Arabic. Capterra lists its availability as Egypt (Search summary; the Capterra page returned 403 when opened) — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Business/How-Egypt-s-Dentolize-is-Quietly-Reshaping-Healthcare-SaaS-Worldwide); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10006711/Dentolize/)
- Dentronix is listed as supporting Arabic and English, serving dentists and multi-branch practices in Iraq and the Middle East (Search summary, directory listing) — [Capterra UAE](https://www.capterra.ae/software/1097553/Dentronix)

### Inferences
- A dental chair behaves like a room resource, so the same resource model can serve both specialties. In dental the chair often becomes the calendar column, so the day view should eventually be able to group by resource as well as by doctor.
- Recall intervals must be per patient, not fixed at six months. The evidence supports flexible intervals, and Open Dental already works this way.
- Full charting and insurance estimates are the costly, regulated part of dental software. Limitsiz does not need them to serve small clinics early. In Jordan, US-style benefit estimation is likely not transferable, but I did not research Jordanian dental insurance.
- Dentolize and Dentronix are the nearest regional rivals on language. Both need a closer look before Limitsiz claims to be the Arabic option.

### Gaps
- Reactivation: I found no vendor documentation describing a reactivation list in Dentrix, Curve, tab32 or Dentally. Open Dental's recall list is the nearest documented tool.
- tab32 and Dentally features were not read. Dentally's pricing URL returned 404 at the path I tried.
- Dentolize's Arabic interface and feature list are unconfirmed.
- Jordan or Gulf dental practice norms for recall, and how far insurers cover dental, were not researched.

## What features are shared by both specialties and would serve dermatology first without blocking dental later?

### Takeaway
Five building blocks are shared: resources, services that require them, multi-visit plans, recall due dates and service-linked forms. Build them with neutral names and dermatology works first, while dental reuses them for chairs, treatment plans and hygiene recall.

### Cited Findings
- Resources attached to services are documented in aesthetics products — [Mangomint](https://www.mangomint.com/learn/help-articles/calendar/resources.md), [Pabau](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/resources-rooms/equipment) — and operatories are required setup in dental — [Open Dental](https://opendental.com/Manual/recall.html)
- Multi-visit work appears as packages in aesthetics — [Pabau](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-repeat-appointments-for-package-sessions) — and as treatment plans in dental — [Dentrix](https://blog.dentrixenterprise.com/following-up-on-your-unscheduled-treatment-plans)
- Recall types with intervals and working lists are documented in dental — [Open Dental](https://opendental.com/Manual/recalltypes.html). Aesthetic intervals are service specific — [Dermapure](https://www.dermapure.com/en/timeline-laser-hair-removal/)
- Forms linked to services — [Pabau](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/medical-form-best-practices)

| Building block | Dermatology and aesthetics use | Dental use later | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resource with a type (room, device, chair) | Laser, treatment room | Operatory | Strong in help docs |
| Service lists required resources | Laser needs laser room | Hygiene needs hygiene chair | Strong in help docs |
| Plan or course: ordered visits, count, interval, optional expiry | Six laser sessions | Crown then fit visit | Good for packages; dental link is my mapping |
| Recall: patient, service, interval, due date, status | Next laser or Botox review | Hygiene and perio recall | Strong for dental; thin for aesthetics |
| Unbooked work list | Sessions sold with no booking | Unscheduled treatment | Dental documented; aesthetic use is my mapping |
| Form or consent linked to a service | Laser consent, photo consent | Treatment consent | Documented for Pabau |
| Before-and-after photos | Core for aesthetics | Useful but secondary | Marketing level |

### Inferences
**What specialty clinics need that a general calendar lacks:** bookings that check a room or device, multi-visit plans, a list of who is due back, unbooked planned work, and consent tied to the service.

Suggested order for Limitsiz, based on evidence and on what the product already has:
1. Resources and service requirements, enforced in staff booking and the WhatsApp agent.
2. Courses: sessions planned, booked, done and remaining, without payments at first.
3. Recall list with interval and due date. The WhatsApp agent can work this list, which fits Limitsiz's strongest asset. Open Dental sells patient self-booking of recall as a $75 add-on, which shows it is priced separately there; I did not confirm this for other vendors.
4. Consent forms linked to services, with a clear "not yet signed" warning.
5. Photos, memberships, commission and retail later. Photos need a privacy decision first.

Naming advice: use "Resource" and "Treatment plan" or "Course" in the data model rather than "Laser" or "Package", so dental is not blocked. Keep charting, claims and insurance estimates out of the model for now, but let a plan's visits link to a future chart item.

### Gaps
- I found no study that ranks these features by what small clinics actually pay for. The order above is my judgment.
- No interviews with Jordanian clinics are in this evidence. Jordanian or Gulf dermatology workflows may differ, especially on deposits and packages.

## What revenue or retention results are reported for recall, packages and rebooking?

### Takeaway
Nearly every number comes from a vendor with something to sell, and most are from North America. They show direction only, and none proves that a feature caused the result. The most useful figures are Zenoti's cancellation figures and Dental Intelligence's reappointment benchmark, and neither is an outcome study.

### Cited Findings

| Figure | Produced by | Scope and caveats |
|---|---|---|
| 37% of appointments cancelled where a medspa guest was rebooked once; 4% where rebooked two or more times | Zenoti (vendor) | Anonymised Zenoti North America data, 2025. No sample size given; Zenoti calls the figures directional. Read — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/the-2026-beauty-and-wellness-benchmark-report-medspa-edition) |
| Overall medspa cancellation rate improved from 16% to 14% | Zenoti | Same source |
| Medspa membership sales +13% year on year; new guest visits −11%; existing guest visits −2% | Zenoti | Same source. New guest figure is described elsewhere as same-store |
| After one year on Boulevard: revenue +30%, retail +31%, packages +36%, memberships +175%, online booking rate +28%, no-show rate −14% | Boulevard (vendor) | "Nearly 100 medspas", own customer data, announced 31 Oct 2023. No control group or baseline described. Read — [Boulevard](https://www.joinblvd.com/newsroom/boulevard-study-shows-business-impact-of-client-experience-platform-on) |
| Hygiene reappointment: 81% average, 94% top 10%, 61% bottom 10%, 2024 | Dental Intelligence (vendor), via Webtonic (marketing agency) | Secondhand; the original attachment was not opened. Read — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/dental-lifecycle-and-retention-marketing-statistics) |
| Treatment dollar acceptance: 39% average, 58% top 10%, 22% bottom 10%, 2024 | Dental Intelligence, via Webtonic | Same source. Webtonic also cites Henry Schein One at 42% and 75%, a different metric |
| Recommended floor of 85% hygiene reappointment | ACT Dental (consultancy) | A recommendation, not a measurement. Search summary — [ACT Dental](https://www.actdental.com/blog/data-snapshot-hygiene-re-appointment-percentage) |
| Rebooking targets of 60%+ for existing clients and 40%+ for new; membership conversion of 10 to 35%+ | Prospyr (vendor blog) | No source stated. Search summary — [Prospyr](https://prospyrmed.com/blog/post/med-spa-kpis-to-track-benchmark-formulas) |

- Dental Intelligence defines hygiene reappointment as the share of hygiene patients who left a hygiene visit with a future hygiene appointment booked (Read) — [Dental Intel](https://educate.dentalintel.com/en/articles/12579825-data-driven-benchmarks-to-guide-your-practice-growth)

### Inferences
- The Zenoti gap (37% vs 4%) probably reflects selection as well as behaviour: guests who rebook repeatedly are already loyal. It supports confirming a first rebooking, which the WhatsApp agent could do. It does not prove that doing so reduces cancellations.
- The Boulevard figures come from customers who chose to switch and may have been growing anyway. Do not use them as a promise or in Limitsiz's marketing.
- Dental Intelligence's reappointment benchmark suggests that booking the next visit before the patient leaves is the standard measure. Limitsiz could report the same measure for free, from data it already has.
- For the "Target Results" section of the site, these numbers are not safe to borrow. Keep them as background unless Limitsiz has its own results.

### Gaps
- Independent or peer-reviewed evidence on revenue effects of recall, packages or rebooking in private aesthetics: none found.
- AmSpa's own State of the Industry report was not reached. Only a secondhand summary appeared.
- Blogs in the dental search gave recall figures that were not verified, including average recall of 55 to 70% and 80 to 90% recall for pre-booked patients against 35 to 45% for others, plus a Canadian estimate of CAD 50,000 to 100,000 a year from a 20-point recall gain. Sourcing was unclear and the pages were not opened, so do not quote them — [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-recall-reactivation-statistics-benchmarks), [EBIKO](https://ebiko.ca/blogs/business-4-dentist/how-to-fix-your-dental-recare-rate-and-stop-losing-thousands-in-hygiene-revenue), [DentalBase](https://www.dentalbase.ai/blogs/dental-recall-system-get-patients-to-come-back)
- No Middle East figures were found for any of these measures.

### Inferences
- A one-location, three-user clinic would pay about $150 a month on Mangomint and $143 on Boulevard Essentials, before messaging and card fees. That is my arithmetic from list prices, and I assumed Mangomint's user fee applies to all three users.
- These prices were built for US and UK clinics. They give a ceiling for Limitsiz's pricing, not a Jordanian market rate.
- Note that messaging is charged separately by several vendors, which is relevant to how Limitsiz prices its WhatsApp agent.
