# Close the inbox gap, then specialise

Limitsiz Clinic Booking is competitive on the core promise, because the WhatsApp agent and the staff calendar share one set of free times, but it is behind the closest rivals on what happens when the agent needs a person. **The first thing to add is a hand-off inbox with staff replies inside the Booking app.** Then finish the reminder and safety work, and report results honestly. After that, build the features that make a dermatology clinic choose you: room and device booking, multi-session courses and consent forms. **The most important competitor is Iyadati AI**, a WhatsApp receptionist focused on Jordan that claims Jordanian dialect, waitlist offers, a staff dashboard and voice notes, although nothing independent confirms these claims and it publishes no prices ([Iyadati AI](https://www.iyadati.ai/)). Arabic alone will not set you apart, since at least six vendors claim it. The opening I can see is depth: no Arabic AI vendor in the research claims device scheduling or course tracking, while the specialist systems that do offer them show no sign of Arabic WhatsApp booking. That is absence of evidence, so check it in a live Iyadati demo before you use it in sales. The ranking is judgement built on thin local data: no source counts Jordan's private clinics, measures how they book, or gives a private-clinic no-show rate. Treat the first pilots as evidence-gathering. All findings are as of 6 November 2026, and this is not legal advice.

## Nineteen ranked features, led by the hand-off inbox

Nothing below reaches a patient until WhatsApp is connected, so Meta setup is the gate in front of the whole list.

**How to read the table.** Ranks combine competitive need, patient safety, evidence and cost. Evidence rates the proof behind the feature. *Strong* means independent studies. *Medium* means competitors' help documents, platform rules or a named public case, which show that a feature exists or is required but not that it pays. *Weak* means vendor marketing, one source or reasoning alone. Effort is my rough estimate for your current small team, not a figure from the notes: S is under a week, M is one to three weeks, L is one to two months. "Who has it" reports what vendors claim or document, not what was tested.

**Where the code goes beyond the research brief.** I checked the repository for effort estimates and found more than the brief listed. An early staff screen in the agent already has a hand-off list, reply box, Take over and Hand back to the AI, on one shared password. Reminders are built at 24 hours and 2 hours with Confirm and Change buttons. Clear emergency words get a fixed reply, and changes wait for the patient's yes (`agent/README.md`). The Booking app records walk-ins, completed visits and no-shows (`booking/client/src/i18n.tsx`). Rows 1 to 4 are therefore smaller than a fresh build.

### Before the first real patient

**1. Hand-off inbox.** Rivals pair the agent with a staff view, and this is where you fall furthest behind. Artera has the best-documented version: a failed request passes to a person with a summary, and a live transcript sits in a shared inbox ([Artera](https://knowledge.artera.io/use-cases-and-add-ons/ai-agents)). Mawidi lets staff take over and pause automated replies ([Mawidi](https://www.mawidi.com)). Zavis claims hand-over in under ten seconds, which is a vendor claim ([Zavis](https://www.zavis.ai/guides/clinic-front-desk-automation-uae)). The early `/staff` screen should move into Booking with named logins, roles and the audit log, and it should show patient photos, which it cannot do today. Add a status for each chat, the reason for the hand-off, a summary, a timer for WhatsApp's 24-hour reply window, a wait time told to the patient, and an alert when nobody answers. Two more reasons make this urgent. UK research found that new channels can add to receptionists' work instead of replacing old ones ([BJGP 2025](https://bjgp.org/content/75/759/e703)). Your own setup guide also says a number moved to the WhatsApp API can no longer use the phone app (`agent/META_SETUP.md`), which leaves the inbox as the secretary's main place to reply. Iyadati says it uses the clinic's existing number, so ask how. **Plan for about one chat in three needing a person at first**, not one in six; the notes explain why in the disagreements section.

**2. Reminders.** The evidence here is the best in the whole study. Cochrane found attendance of about 68% with no reminder and 79% with text, with low to moderate quality evidence ([Cochrane](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/1386850/1/CD007458.pdf)). A 2026 review of hospital trials also favours reminders, though its text-message subgroup alone was not statistically significant ([Al-Turbag et al.](https://jhmhp.amegroups.org/article/view/10215/html)). The only WhatsApp study covers 475,214 appointments in Buenos Aires, with no-shows at 34.6% without reminders. One reminder 24 hours before cut no-shows by about 8 points, and pairing 72-hour and 24-hour reminders did no better; it is a preprint, not randomised ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26360609v3)). An older review favoured multiple notifications ([Robotham et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Using-digital-notifications-to-improve-attendance-Robotham-Satkunanathan/a68086634bbd21a439342b7bd43ef7376779cfa5)), so the number is unsettled. Keep 24 hours as the core reminder and treat your 2-hour one as something to test. Add a direct Cancel path, because reminders raise cancellations and those slots need capturing. Keep the text to name, date, time, doctor and clinic. **Promise a modest result:** the research note's estimate is 5 to 10 points at a high no-show rate and perhaps 2 to 3 points near 10%. Vendor figures such as Phreesia's 79% have no stated baseline on the page read ([Phreesia](https://www.phreesia.com/patient-appointment-reminders/)), and Clinicy's reported 85% comes from a page where one testimonial is labelled Example ([Clinicy](https://clinicy.com.sa)).

**3. Safety rules.** The clearest public failure was a stroke patient who could not get past an AI receptionist at an English GP surgery, moved to another practice, and saw the surgery remove the system ([Digital Trends, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/ai-receptionists-seem-like-a-dangerous-idea-after-a-patient-reveals-it-failed-to-understand-her-request/)). WhatsApp's policy also requires a clear route to a person when you automate ([WhatsApp policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). Much of this is built: AI disclosure instructions, no medical advice, fixed emergency text, a held change until the patient says yes, and hand-off after too many failed steps. What remains is booking for family members, which your README lists as not handled, and which matters because a 2026 benchmark treats booking the wrong patient as critical ([PatientAgentBench, snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25485)). Also add a stop after two misunderstandings, testing of Arabic words for "a person", a clinic-set after-hours message, and the full 51-case run with sign-off. Keep the agent strictly about booking, because general-purpose AI assistants have reportedly been barred from the WhatsApp platform since 15 January 2026 ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban)).

**4. Results report.** Vendor headline numbers are self-reported and not comparable. One respected vendor's own first month showed 30% of calls resolved without a person ([Hello Patient, April 2026](https://www.hellopatient.com/post/pulse-md-urgent-care)), and a vendor post argues that "contained" chats can hide harm ([Druid AI](https://www.druidai.com/blog/dont-let-the-containment-dashboard-pick-your-healthcare-ai-agent)). Show chats handled, bookings by the agent including after hours, visits that actually happened, no-show rate, hand-offs by reason and response time, booking errors found, and any revenue estimate with its sum shown. Record a tally of calls, messages and no-shows for a week before go-live. That baseline would be stronger than anything published for this market.

### Needed to win dermatology clinics

**5. Rooms and devices.** A booking in a laser clinic needs a doctor, a room and a machine, and your "one booking per doctor" rule cannot see the machine. Mangomint's design is the one to copy: a resource is a thing, and a service lists what it requires ([Mangomint](https://www.mangomint.com/learn/help-articles/calendar/resources.md)). Pabau blocks moving a laser booking to a room without the machine ([Pabau](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/why-room-changes-are-restricted-for-appointments)), and Jane keeps rooms and equipment on its top plan ([Jane](https://jane.app/pricing)). Dental chairs are required setup in Open Dental, so the same model carries over ([Open Dental](https://opendental.com/Manual/recall.html)). Name it "resource", not "laser". Effort is large because the free-time logic exists in both the agent and Booking and must stay identical.

**6. Dialect test.** Iyadati claims Jordanian dialect, Thamra claims Levantine, and Basma claims 94% dialect accuracy, all self-reported ([Thamra](https://thamragroup.com/); [Basma](https://basma-app.pages.dev/)). No independent test exists. A published test, using real Jordanian phrasing graded by Jordanian clinic staff and showing the number of cases, would be rare in this market. It needs little new code because the test harness exists.

**7. Week view.** The research did not confirm a week view at any vendor; Cliniko's page says only that its display is adjustable ([Cliniko](https://www.cliniko.com/features/appointments/)). It ranks here because it is cheap and a secretary planning a course of visits needs it, not because evidence demands it.

**8. Courses and repeat visits.** Build one feature that covers both recurring appointments and treatment courses. Pabau books repeat sessions from a package, limited by sessions left and expiry ([Pabau](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/how-to-repeat-appointments-for-package-sessions)). Cliniko offers recurring intervals, and Practo schedules a course in one click ([Cliniko](https://www.cliniko.com/features/appointments/); [Practo Ray](https://www.practo.com/ray)). Start with sessions planned, booked, done and remaining, with no payments. Let clinics set intervals per service, because published laser intervals vary and come from clinic marketing, not guidelines ([Dermapure](https://www.dermapure.com/en/timeline-laser-hair-removal/)). **One catch:** the agent's demo limits of 30 days ahead and 3 upcoming bookings would block a six-session laser course, so course bookings need their own rule. Do not advertise package discounts through the product, because Jordan Medical Association instructions bar offers without committee approval ([Al-Wakeel](https://www.alwakeelnews.com/Section_1/%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A_714402)).

**9 and 10. Insurer status and cash receipts.** On 18 October 2026 the Jordan Medical Association told private doctors to stop dealing with four insurers, including MedNet, and to take cash with a receipt, while insurers threatened legal action and said they are studying an end to medical networks ([Maqar](https://maqar.com/archives/774461); [FactJo](https://factjo.com/news/150879?slug=nkab-alatbaaa-tokf-taaaml-alktaaa-alkhas-maa-4-shrkat-tamyn-othyk-1791994665)). The latest report found is dated 17 October, so the position may have changed. A clinic-kept list per doctor (accepted, cash and receipt, not accepted, with an effective date) lets the agent answer correctly and takes minutes to change. A receipt the patient can take to the insurer is cheap and keeps its value if insurers move to pay-and-claim. Dentists are not covered by these reports, because the dental association's position was not researched. Cosmetic work is probably uninsured, which is an inference, so this ranks by urgency and not by depth of need.

**11. Consent forms.** Pabau advises linking every form to a service, and it can chase unsigned forms ([Pabau](https://support.pabau.com/en/pabau2/medical-form-best-practices); [Pabau marketing, vendor claim](https://teststaging.pabau.com/features/digital-forms)). A "not yet signed" warning on the booking is the useful part. Send forms by secure web link, not in chat, given the Meta question below, and keep separate tick-boxes for treatment and for photo use, as Pabau's template does ([Pabau](https://pabau.com/templates/photo-release-form/)).

**12. Waitlist.** Automatic slot offers are confirmed only at Jane's top plan, NexHealth and Luma, and Cliniko's is manual ([Jane](https://jane.app/guide/setting-up-your-wait-list-notifications); [NexHealth](https://www.nexhealth.com/features/scheduling); [Cliniko](https://help.cliniko.com/en/articles/1402450-using-the-wait-list)). Iyadati already claims it. No trial of any waitlist was found. Luma's reported 42% fill rate is a vendor case study ([Luma](https://www.casestudies.com/company/luma-health/case-study/first-choice-health-centers-gained-127k-in-additional-revenue-from-lumas-smart-waitlist)). Copy Jane's staff buffer before a slot goes out, and send a test template to Meta first, because the offer may be classed as marketing.

**13. Risk list and repeat no-show block.** A review found phone calls to patients at predicted risk cut no-shows in three trials, typically by about 39% ([JAMIA, snippet](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/30/3/559/6889491)). Dermatology data names long lead time and past no-shows as the main predictors ([study, snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369597606_Understanding_Barriers_to_Medical_Appointment_Keeping_A_Case-control_Study_of_Predictive_Factors_for_No-shows_and_Same-day_Cancellations_in_Dermatology_Clinics_in_an_Academic_Medical_Center_in_the_Uni)). A plain daily list using past no-show, first visit and long lead time needs no AI. I would leave age and sex out of the flag, even though the studies link them, because staff should not treat patients differently by group. Doctolib also lets practitioners block repeat no-shows from online booking and applies this to its phone assistant ([Le Quotidien](https://medecine-generale.lequotidiendumedecin.fr/liberal-soins-de-ville/gestion-cabinet/doctolib-annonce-des-mesures-supplementaires-contre-les-rendez-vous-non-honores); [Doctolib community](https://community.doctolib.fr/t/les-details-qui-font-la-difference-avril-a-juin-2026/184920)).

### After the first pilots

**14. Recall.** Open Dental runs recall as a working list with intervals set per patient ([Open Dental](https://opendental.com/Manual/recalllist.html)), and sells patient self-booking of recall for $75 a month per location (6 Nov 2026) ([fees](https://www.opendental.com/site/fees.html)). Fixed six-month recall is not supported by the evidence, as NICE allows 3 to 24 months by risk ([NICE](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/cg19/resources/2020-exceptional-surveillance-of-dental-checks-intervals-between-oral-health-reviews-nice-guideline-cg19-pdf-11678930994373)). Begin as a list staff phone from. Automated WhatsApp recall is likely to count as marketing, costing more and needing separate consent, and that is a question for your lawyer.

**15. Voice notes.** The agent answers "please type" today, and Iyadati claims it understands them. One audit, as summarised by a vendor, found about 1% of transcriptions wholly invented ([Frenchy Digital](https://frenchydigital.com/blog/ai-voice-agents-medical-front-desk-2026)). If you add this, confirm every detail back in text. Meanwhile, text is a real advantage for patients with speech problems.

**16. Deposits.** Mawidi, Jane, Cliniko, NexHealth and Pabau all sell deposits, but no randomised study of them was found, and a Danish trial of a roughly €34 fine showed no difference, at about 5% non-attendance in both groups ([Blæhr et al.](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Blaehr%22%20AND%20fine%20AND%20BMJ%20Open&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=3)). Whether Jordanian patients accept deposits is unknown. CliQ is widely used, with 167.9 million transactions in 2025 ([Int@j](https://intaj.net/jopacc-releases-its-2025-payments-systems-report/)), but no public business interface was found. Test it by hand: staff send the clinic's CliQ alias for long or costly procedures, then mark it paid. Banks have charged business accounts CliQ fees since 1 October 2025 ([Roya](https://en.royanews.tv/news/63722/Jordan-introduces-fees-on-%E2%80%98CliQ%E2%80%99-transfers-for-merchants,-businesses)).

**17 to 19. Web link, dental views, photos.** Self-booking is standard among direct peers, and a web link gives clinics a route that does not depend on Meta. Dentrix's report of planned treatment with no appointment is the idea to borrow for dental, and it also works for unbooked laser sessions ([Dentrix](https://blog.dentrixenterprise.com/following-up-on-your-unscheduled-treatment-plans)). Photos come last because they need a privacy decision first. Jordan's medical advertising rules already require written consent for before-and-after images and ban images taken during procedures ([Al-Wakeel](https://www.alwakeelnews.com/Section_1/%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B7%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A_714402)).

### What not to build yet

**Live insurance verification** is not realistic: no public insurer or administrator interface was found and the system itself is in dispute. Your website still promises an agent that "checks eligibility and coverage" (`app/client/src/translations.ts`), and I recommend rewording it before a clinic asks. **Reading insurance cards from photos** is Iyadati's claim, but WhatsApp's policy bars asking for personal ID and financial account numbers ([WhatsApp policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)), so get legal advice first. **A link to Hakeem** has no known route for private clinics ([Petra](https://petra.gov.jo/index.php/en/news/hakeem-system-expands-to-cover-482-healthcare-facilities-so-far-ceo)). **Your own invoicing** can wait: e-invoicing under JoFotara has applied since 1 April 2025, but it is unclear whether clinics qualify, and Qoyod already sells a JoFotara-linked product ([Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/fields/accounting-software-for-clinics-jordan/)), so ask an accountant and consider connecting to it. **Promotion broadcasts** risk breaching medical advertising rules. **Automatic overbooking** has uncertain evidence ([JAMIA, snippet](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/30/3/559/6889491)). **Memberships, commission and retail** belong to a billing product you do not have, and no reliable documentation on commission was found. **Card gateways** come only after confirmed Jordan quotes; third-party guides cite about 2.5% to 3.5% per card payment, unverified and undated ([PayAtlas](https://payatlas.com/countries/jordan-jo)).

These rates come from a third party, so confirm them in WhatsApp Manager. On those rates, a clinic with 400 bookings a month, two reminders and six agent replies per booking would pay about $20 a month, and one with 1,500 bookings about $100. This is my arithmetic, not vendor data. **Your own setup guide still says replies within 24 hours are free** (`agent/META_SETUP.md`), which is out of date, and Meta's 30 September deadline for adding a payment method has passed, so add one at connection.

Three pricing lessons follow, and all are judgement. The AI agent, not the calendar, must justify any price above the $10 anchor. A flat fee beats a per-booking fee, because doctors resent paying for bookings that never attend ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.zocdoc.com?page=4)). Do not move features to a higher tier later, which is the main SimplePractice complaint ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/130710/SimplePractice/reviews/?page=8)). Separately, give each clinic its own number, because each number gets its own 1,000 free replies and template penalties can spread across a shared account ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/template-categorization)).

## The Meta health rule needs a lawyer before launch

The two notes disagree, and this cannot be settled from the notes alone.

| | `ai_front_desk_whatsapp.md` | `jordan_market_evidence.md` |
|---|---|---|
| Wording | The telemedicine and health-information ban applies only where regulations prohibit distributing health data to systems without heightened protections; no region named (paraphrase in the note) | "Don't use WhatsApp for telemedicine or to send or request any health related information." (quoted in the note) |
| Effect | Depends on Jordanian law | Flat ban, everywhere |
| Version given | Policy last updated 23 September 2026 | No date given |
| Source | [WhatsApp policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) | Same page, marked as read |

**On the notes, the conditional wording is the better candidate for the current text**, because it is the only one tied to a version date, and the short quote reads like the opening of a longer sentence. That second point is my inference. It is not proof, because both notes cite the same page, on the same research date, and neither reproduces a full clause with a condition. Even if the conditional wording is right, the question moves to Jordanian law, which that note did not research. The data protection law treats health information as sensitive ([MoDEE, Article 2](https://www.modee.gov.jo/ebv4.0/root_storage/en/eb_list_page/pdpl.pdf)), but the notes do not say whether that amounts to a ban on such systems. No record of Meta enforcing the clause against a clinic was found. **Flag this for legal review, and have the lawyer read the live clause.**

The safe course is to design for the stricter reading until you have that answer, because the cost is small. Reminders stay free of health content. The agent never asks what is wrong, and medical questions go to staff, as the agent already does. Three points need a specific ruling. In dermatology the service name itself can reveal health information, and the agent names the service when booking and reading back. Voice notes and photos carry whatever patients send. Forms should go by web link. Section 4 of the policy also needs reading: one note calls medical and healthcare products restricted, the other prohibited, and either could affect clinics promoting cosmetic products.

The same lawyer should cover several Jordanian questions: whether Limitsiz is a controller or processor; whether reminders sent through you fit the healthcare exception in Article 6; whether Meta, AI providers and servers abroad satisfy Article 15, given that no list of adequate countries was found; whether a sole-doctor clinic needs a data protection officer under Article 11; and whether recall messages count as healthcare, marketing or advertising under medical and dental association rules ([MoDEE](https://www.modee.gov.jo/ebv4.0/root_storage/en/eb_list_page/pdpl.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which features are missing?" to "which claims can you prove?".
